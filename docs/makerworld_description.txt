BLIPR - DIY 24 GHz Radar Chronograph (case v0.12)

BLIPR (Ballistic Launch Inspection Portable Radar) is a Doppler radar chronograph you build yourself. It sits on the bench beside your rifle, measures each shot's muzzle velocity and sends it to Range Book over Bluetooth. Nothing goes downrange.

This is the 3D-printed case. The electronics (circuit board, firmware, parts list, build guide) are free on GitHub, and there's a full step-by-step assembly video on YouTube.

- Build video: https://youtu.be/nRWELvyEKN8
- GitHub (board files, firmware, parts list, build guide): https://github.com/saoneill01-a11y/rangebook-blipr
- Range Book app: https://rangebook.org

WHAT'S IN THE CASE
- Radar pocket behind a thin replaceable front window
- Bay for a single-18650 battery holder (USB-C charging and power button through the side wall)
- Mounts for the 70 x 90 mm BLIPR circuit board, with the Teensy's USB port out the right wall
- Lid with a window for a 0.96" OLED screen
- Optional sensor pod on the lid (temperature, humidity, air pressure, level and compass)
- 1/4-20 tripod mount at the balance point

Size: 118.8 x 130.8 mm, 38.8 mm tall with the lid.

PARTS
- base
- lid: pick ONE
    lid_oled: screen window (the usual one)
    lid_sensors: screen window plus mounts for the sensor pod
    lid_plain: no screen
- front_cover: the radar window. Print in natural, white or gray, NOT black or any filled filament (carbon, glitter, metal), or the radar can't see through it
- sensor_pod and sensor_pod_roof: only with lid_sensors

PRINT SETTINGS (PETG recommended; no supports needed)
- base: floor on the bed. 0.2 mm layers, 6 walls, 4 top/bottom layers, 25% gyroid infill. Use a brim.
- lid: top face on the bed (the lettering comes out crisp). Same settings as the base.
- front_cover: flat smooth face on the bed. 0.15 mm layers, 100% infill. The window is 0.9 mm thick.
- Material: PETG works well for most people. ASA is best if it will sit in strong sun or a hot car, or you live somewhere
  really hot (it warps, so use an enclosure or draft shield and a bed around 100 C). PLA is fine for a test fit, but it
  softens in a hot car. The test unit was printed in PLA.

HARDWARE
- 8 x M3 heat-set inserts (about 4.6-5 mm OD, up to 7 mm long): 4 lid corners + 4 board posts (2 more for the sensor pod)
- 1 x 1/4-20 heat-set insert, 13 mm long or shorter (tripod)
- 4 x M3 x 8 mm screws (lid), 4 x M3 x 6 mm pan-head screws (board)
- 4 x M3 x 6 mm self-tapping screws (battery holder)
- 4 x M2 x 4 mm screws (screen)
- 1 mm x 5 mm foam tape and a little hot glue for the radar

The full parts list, wiring, soldering steps and electrical checks are in the build guide on GitHub. BLIPR is a hobby project and still being tested: it has been tested on .22 LR and .38 Special (within about 3 fps of an Athlon radar chronograph on the same shots), with supersonic rifle ammo next.
